Naan Recipe

Ingredients

  • All-Purpose Flour (Maida): 2 cups
  • Yogurt: 1/4 cup (provides softness and slight tang).
  • Warm Water/Milk: 1/2 cup (milk makes the naan richer and softer).
  • Sugar: 1 teaspoon (helps activate yeast and aids browning).
  • Salt: 1/2 teaspoon.
  • Instant Yeast: 1 teaspoon (or 1/2 tsp baking powder + 1/4 tsp baking soda for a quicker, non-yeast version).
  • Oil/Ghee: 1 tablespoon (in the dough).
  • Extra Ghee/Butter: For brushing the cooked naan.
    • Optional Toppings: Minced garlic, nigella seeds (kalonji), or chopped cilantro.

Step-by-Step Recipe

  1. Activate the Yeast

    2. In a small bowl, mix the warm water/milk, sugar, and yeast. Let it sit for 5–10 minutes until it becomes frothy. (If using the baking powder/soda method, skip this step and mix those directly into the dry flour.)

  2. Make the Dough

    3. In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour, salt, yogurt, oil/ghee, and the yeast mixture. Knead for 5-7 minutes until you have a smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky dough. If it's too sticky, add a dusting of flour.

  3. Proof the Dough

    4. Lightly grease the dough ball with a little oil, place it in a bowl, and cover it with a damp cloth. Let it rest in a warm place for 1 to 2 hours (until it doubles in size).

  4. Shape the Naan

    5. Punch down the dough to release the air. Divide it into 6–8 equal portions and roll them into balls. On a lightly floured surface, roll each ball into an oval or teardrop shape, about 1/4 inch thick.

  5. Add Toppings

    6. If you want garlic or herb naan, sprinkle the minced garlic and cilantro over the rolled-out dough and use your rolling pin to gently press them into the surface.

  6. Cook the Naan

    7. Heat your cast-iron skillet or heavy-bottomed pan over high heat until it is very hot.
    Pro-tip: Lightly brush the back of the rolled dough with a little water. Place the water-side down on the hot pan.
    Cover the pan with a lid for about 30 seconds. You will see bubbles forming on the surface.
    Once the bottom is browned, remove the lid. If the top isn't charred enough, you can either flip the pan upside down over the open gas flame (if using a gas stove) to char the top, or just use tongs to hold the naan over the flame for a few seconds.

  7. Finish and Serve

    8. Immediately brush the hot naan with plenty of butter or ghee. Serve immediately while warm—naan is best eaten right off the heat.
